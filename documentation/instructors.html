    Instructor workspace

    How a student becomes an instructor, what the teaching dashboard contains, how a course is built and published, and how earnings turn into a payout request.

    Nine sections. Every screen here is behind the instructor role and the instructor-active check; an administrator controls who gets the role and can suspend it without deleting the account.

    Becoming an instructor

    Nobody can publish a course immediately after registering. Teaching is an application, and an administrator decides. The whole flow lives at /dashboard/become-instructor.

    1. Apply from the dashboard

      A student opens Account → Become Instructor and fills in:

      • Expertise — a short label such as "Web Development" or "UI/UX Design", up to 255 characters.
      • Bio — at least 50 characters; the form validates this before it submits. This is the pitch an administrator reads.
      • Social links (optional) — website, LinkedIn, X (Twitter), and YouTube. Each must be a valid URL.

    2. Wait for review

      The page switches to Application under review with a pending status. The account keeps working as a student in the meantime. The application appears in the admin panel under Users → Instructor Applications.

    3. Get approved

      Approval turns the account's role into instructor, creates the public teaching profile from the application, and sends an email plus an in-app notification with a link to /dashboard/teaching. The Teaching and Earnings groups appear in the sidebar; Become Instructor disappears.

    4. Handle rejection or a later suspension

      A rejection records the administrator's note, which is shown on the application page together with an Apply Again button. The student edits the same form and resubmits; the previous note stays in the admin panel for context.

    Banned is not deleted

    An administrator can set an instructor profile to banned (workspace locked, existing courses stay public) or rejected (sign-in blocked and courses hidden). Neither deletes the account or its courses, and both are reversible from Users → All Instructors.

    The teaching dashboard

    /dashboard answers one question well: how are my courses doing? Every figure is scoped to the signed-in instructor, never to the whole marketplace. The date-range control applies to the cards and the three charts; the course and recent-sale panels show their own latest data.

    Panel What it shows
    Headline statisticsSeven cards: total revenue, total sales, total students, total courses, paid earnings, pending earnings, and available balance. Revenue, sales, and students carry the percentage change against the previous period.
    Revenue chartNet earnings over the selected period, from the same ledger used by payouts.
    Sales and enrollment chartsSales per day and new enrollments per day, with a cumulative enrollment line that separates a steadily growing course from a new one.
    Course performanceThe top five courses by revenue or sales — switch the ordering in the panel header — with sales, students, rating, and revenue.
    Recent salesThe latest purchases with student, course, gross amount, your earnings, date, and payment status.

    Creating a course

    Start at Teaching → Create Course. The builder is a seven-step form with a step rail on the left; every step validates as you leave it, the completed steps get a check mark, and you can jump back at any time without losing the draft.

    Step 1 — Basic Information

    Course titleAt least 8 characters, up to 80. This is the headline in the catalog and the course page.
    Short descriptionOne sentence, up to 160 characters, used on cards and in search results.
    Full descriptionRich text with headings, lists, links, and images. It becomes the Overview tab.
    CategoryOne category, including subcategories shown as "Parent / Child". The list comes from the admin panel.
    LevelBeginner, intermediate, or advanced.
    Course statusOngoing while you are still adding lessons; Completed when the course is finished. This is the lifecycle that unlocks certificates — students cannot receive one while the course is ongoing, even at 100% progress.

    Step 2 — Course Details

    Learning objectives (at least one is required), requirements, and tags. Objectives and requirements are lists shown on the course page; tags help discovery. Write objectives as outcomes — "Build a checkout flow with Stripe" rather than "Stripe".

    Step 3 — Curriculum

    Sections and lessons, both reorderable. A course needs at least one section with at least one lesson before it can be submitted, and every new lesson needs a video file.

    Lesson field Meaning
    TitleThe label in the outline and the player.
    DurationMinutes. It sets the course's total runtime and the 80% watched threshold that auto-completes a lesson.
    VideoUploaded to the configured media disk. Upload progress is reported per file.
    Free previewLets a visitor watch this lesson before buying. Use it on the lesson that best demonstrates your teaching.
    AttachmentsSlides, exercise files, datasets, and source archives — up to 20 files of 50 MB each per lesson (PDF, ZIP, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, plain text). Downloads are authorized per enrolled student, so a copied link does not work for anyone else.

    Files upload while the course is saved, one after another. The save panel names the file being transferred and shows a percentage, so a large curriculum does not look frozen.

    Step 4 — Pricing

    Price and an optional discounted price, both in the site currency (USD in the seeded settings). The discounted price is the amount a student pays, not an amount off; it must be lower than the list price. Enter 0 for a free course. The step shows the final amount before you save.

    Step 5 — Media

    A thumbnail is required. A preview video is optional but is the strongest conversion asset a course page has: it is the trailer visitors can watch without buying. Both can be replaced later, subject to the edit-request rules below.

    Step 6 — Quiz

    One optional end-of-course quiz. Leave it inactive and empty to skip it; see Building the quiz. A course with no quiz issues certificates once every lesson is complete and the course is marked completed.

    Step 7 — Review & Publish

    A summary of the course — sections, lessons, total runtime, free previews, objectives, requirements, and the quiz — plus a list of any step that still blocks submission, each with a Fix now link. Then two choices:

    • Save as Draft — keeps the course private to you. It does not enter the admin queue and is not for sale.
    • Submit for review — validates every required step and places the course in Course Approvals. You can keep editing while it waits; the public sees nothing until it is approved.

    What the course list shows

    /dashboard/teaching lists your courses in a grid or a table, with search, a status filter (draft, published, ongoing, completed), and sorting by recently updated, highest revenue, or most students. Each card carries two badges: the publication state (draft / published) and the approval state (draft, pending, approved, rejected). The ongoing/completed lifecycle is visible in the editor and can be filtered for in the list.

    The row menu offers View (only for an approved course, and it opens the public page), Edit, and Delete. A course with enrolled students cannot be deleted; the API refuses and the interface explains that support must be contacted.

    Approval, rejection, and editing a live course

    A course cannot appear in the catalog until an administrator approves it. The approval state is visible on every course card and at the top of the editor.

    State Meaning
    DraftPrivate to the instructor. Not submitted, not reviewed, not visible.
    Pending approvalSubmitted and waiting for a decision. Still not for sale.
    ApprovedLive in the catalog, subject to the Active switch.
    RejectedReturned with a reason. Edit the course and submit again.

    Approval and rejection both send an email and an in-app notification. A rejection reason stays visible in the editor until the course is resubmitted.

    Editing after approval

    Once a course is live, its public details are protected. The builder stays editable, but what happens on save depends on the field:

    Field group On save
    Sensitive: title, price, discount price, level, category, thumbnail, preview video Collected into a single pending edit request for the administrator. The course keeps running with its current data until the request is approved. A toast says so explicitly.
    Everything else: description, objectives, requirements, tags, curriculum, lessons, attachments, quiz, and the ongoing/completed status Applied immediately. Students see the new description or lesson the next time they open the course.

    The editor shows three different banners, and they mean different things:

    • Waiting for admin review — a course submitted for the first time, or sensitive changes queued on a live course. The course keeps its current data.
    • This course was rejected — the course itself is not approved; fix it and press Submit for review.
    • Your last change request was rejected — the course is still live with its approved data; edit the fields and save again to create a new request.

    Reverting a field to its live value removes it from the pending request. Approving the request applies the whole batch at once; rejecting it keeps the live values, records the reason, and deletes any replacement media that was uploaded.

    The save button follows the state

    On a new course the footer offers Save as Draft and Submit for review. On an existing course the header offers Save for the current data, and the final step offers Save and submit update (or Submit for review when the course is not yet live).

    Curriculum and attachments

    The curriculum builder is the third step of the course form, and it is organized in two levels. Both are reordered with the up and down controls on each row.

    • Section — a heading that groups lessons, for example "Getting started". Every section needs a title.
    • Lesson — the unit a student watches: video, duration, free-preview flag, and attachments.

    Editing the curriculum of a live course is not a sensitive change: adding a lesson publishes it immediately to enrolled students. That is intentional while a course is ongoing; mark the course completed when the curriculum is final, so students know the course will not grow and certificates can be issued.

    Attachments

    Any lesson can carry downloadable resources alongside the video: slides, exercise files, source code, datasets, or an external link. Uploaded files keep their title, type, and size so students know what they are getting, and each attachment tracks how many times it was downloaded. Downloads go through an endpoint that checks enrollment first; a link copied out of the classroom returns 403 for everyone else.

    Building the quiz

    The quiz step is optional. It writes the assessment that gates the certificate for this course.

    • Activation — an inactive quiz is invisible to students. A course with an active quiz requires a pass before a certificate is issued.
    • Questions — each has its own text, a point value, and an optional explanation that students see when they review a marked attempt.
    • Options — two or more answers per question, with at least one marked correct.
    • Shape — each question holds between two and six options, a point value, and one or more options marked correct.

    Grading happens on the server against the stored correct options. The attempt is saved with the answers as submitted, and the resulting percentage decides whether the certificate requirement is met. The pass mark and attempt limit are supervised by the administrator in Certificates → Quizzes, not by the instructor.

    A failed attempt does not lose progress

    Lessons stay completed when a quiz attempt fails. The student can retry while attempts remain, and the certificate is issued on the first pass once the course itself is completed.

    Earnings

    An instructor is paid per sale, not per month, and the ledger is precise about the arithmetic. /dashboard/earnings shows the summary; /dashboard/earnings/sales shows every line.

    Field Meaning
    Gross amountWhat the student paid for that item.
    CommissionThe site's percentage at the moment of the sale, stored on the record so later rate changes never rewrite history.
    Net amountGross minus commission — what the instructor earned.
    AvailableThe part of the net amount not already tied to a pending, approved, or paid request.
    Statusavailable, requested, paid, or reversed.

    The summary separates available balance, pending balance (requested or approved, not yet paid), and paid. A reversed earning — a refunded order, for example — stays in the ledger rather than being deleted, so the history always explains the current balance. Because earnings are recorded per order item, a multi-course order pays each instructor for exactly their own course.

    Payout requests

    Withdrawing earnings is a request, not an automatic transfer. The instructor asks; an administrator pays out of band and marks it paid. The form is at /dashboard/earnings/payouts.

    Before the request

    • The available balance must be at or above the site's minimum payout amount. Below it, the request button is disabled and the page states the threshold.
    • At least one payout method must be configured by the administrator. The methods and their account hints come from Settings → Payout Methods.
    • The amount cannot exceed the available balance; the dialog validates both the minimum and the maximum before it sends anything.

    The request dialog

    AmountAny value from the minimum up to the available balance. Partial withdrawals are allowed; whatever remains stays available for a later request.
    Payment methodOne of the configured methods (bank transfer, PayPal, Wise, or whatever the administrator added).
    Payment accountWhere the money should go, with a hint that changes with the selected method — an IBAN, a PayPal email, a Wise account number. Required.

    On submit the request is reserved against the oldest available earnings and appears in the history as pending. The available balance drops by the reserved amount; the money is not gone, just allocated.

    What happens next

    1. Review

      The request waits in the admin panel under Payments → Payout Requests. The administrator sees the amount, the method, and the account details you entered, together with your total and available earnings.

    2. Approval

      The reservation is confirmed and the request becomes approved. It is still not paid.

    3. Payment

      The administrator sends the money by the method you chose, then presses Mark Paid. The request becomes paid and the amount appears in your paid total. The platform itself does not move money.

    4. Or rejection

      A rejected request carries a reason, which you can read in the history. The reserved amount is released back to your available balance, and you can request again.

    The history on the same page lists every request with its amount, method, status, date, payment details, and any rejection reason, newest first. It is paged, so a long teaching history does not turn into one endless screen.

    Availability is computed from the ledger

    Available balance is net earnings minus everything already requested or paid — not a separate wallet. A refund that reverses an earning can therefore lower the available balance after a sale, and an unpaid request always shows in the pending total.

    A launch checklist for a new instructor

    1. Fill the teaching profile — bio, expertise, links. It is public at /instructors/{id}.
    2. Create the course with a clear title, a one-sentence short description, and a full description that states the outcome.
    3. Add at least one section and one lesson with a video; mark one lesson as a free preview.
    4. Add a thumbnail and, if possible, a short trailer.
    5. Set the price (or zero for free) and check the final amount.
    6. Write the quiz if the course needs an assessment; activate it only when every question is correct.
    7. Submit for review and respond to any rejection reason.
    8. After approval, keep the course ongoing while adding lessons; mark it completed when the curriculum is final, so students can earn certificates.
    9. Configure a payout method expectation with the site owner, then request a withdrawal once the available balance passes the minimum.
