    Instructor workspace

    How someone becomes an instructor, how they build and publish a course, and how the money they earn turns into a payout.

    Eight sections. Everything here is behind the instructor role; an administrator controls who gets it and what they may change.

    Becoming an instructor

    Nobody can publish a course immediately after registering. Teaching is an application, and an administrator decides.

    1. Apply from the dashboard

      A student opens Become an instructor and fills in three things: a biography, their area of expertise, and any social or portfolio links that back it up. The form states what the site expects, so a serious applicant writes a real pitch rather than one line.

    2. Wait for review

      The application sits in Users → Instructor Applications with a pending status, and the applicant sees that their submission is under review. Their account keeps working as a student in the meantime.

    3. Get approved

      On approval the account's role becomes instructor and an instructor profile is created with the bio, expertise, and links from the application. Courses can then be created, and the instructor workspace appears in the dashboard.

    4. Handle rejection or a later suspension

      A rejection carries an administrator's note, which the applicant can read. An instructor profile can also be set to banned later, which locks the workspace without deleting the account or its courses.

    The instructor dashboard

    The workspace answers one question well: how are my courses doing? Every figure is scoped to the signed-in instructor, never to the whole site.

    Panel What it shows
    Revenue chart Earnings over time, from the same ledger used for payouts.
    Sales chart Number and value of sales per period, with a date range filter.
    Enrollment chart Students joining each course over time, which separates a growing course from a new one.
    Top performing courses The courses earning the most, so an instructor knows where to spend their time.
    Recent sales The latest purchases with student, course, amount, and date.
    Sales history The full list, filterable by course and by date range, with the net amount the instructor actually received after commission.

    An Earnings area holds the balance and payout history, described further down this chapter, and Courses lists everything the instructor has created with its status.

    Creating a course

    Course creation is a five-step form with a step rail, so an instructor can leave and come back without losing the structure. Each step saves, and the course keeps its draft or pending status until final submission.

    1. Basic information

      Course title, a short description for cards and search results, the full description, a category, the level (beginner, intermediate, or advanced), and the course status field used when the course is between ongoing and completed.

    2. Details

      Learning objectives — what a student will be able to do — the requirements they should already have, and tags that help people find the course. Objectives and requirements are stored as lists, and both are shown on the course page.

    3. Media

      A thumbnail and an optional preview video. The preview is the trailer a visitor watches before buying, and the form marks it as the thing that most affects conversion. Thumbnails can be replaced or removed at any time.

    4. Pricing

      Price, an optional discount price, and the currency. The step shows the original price, the discount, and the final amount a student pays, so a discount is never accidentally deeper than intended.

    5. Review and submit

      A summary of the course — sections and lessons, total runtime, how many lessons are free previews, objectives, requirements — followed by submission. Submitting is what sends the course for approval.

    Video uploads report progress and survive a slow connection; a checklist appears while a large file is transferring so the instructor knows the upload is still alive.

    Approval, rejection, and editing a live course

    A course cannot appear in the catalog until an administrator approves it. The status moves through a fixed sequence, and each transition is visible to the instructor.

    Status Meaning
    Draft Being written. Only the instructor sees it.
    Pending approval Submitted and waiting for a decision. It is still not for sale.
    Approved Live in the catalog. Students can find and buy it.
    Rejected Sent back with a reason. The instructor edits and submits again.

    Instructors are notified when a course is approved or rejected, and a rejection reason stays visible on the course until it is resubmitted.

    Changing a course that is already live

    Once a course is approved, its public details are protected. Editing sensitive fields — the ones students see and buy on — does not silently change a live course. Instead the proposed values are collected into a single pending edit request, which an administrator reviews in Courses → Course Edit Requests:

    • All pending changes appear as one batch, so a review is a single decision rather than a queue of small updates.
    • An edit can be reverted field by field while it waits, and an edit that has been reverted is removed from the batch.
    • The course keeps its approved status and stays on sale while the request is open — students never see a course disappear because of an edit.
    • On approval the proposed values become the live ones; on rejection the reason is recorded and the live values stay untouched.

    Curriculum and attachments

    The curriculum builder is where a course becomes teaching material. It is organized in two levels, and both are reorderable.

    Sections and lessons

    A section groups lessons under a heading; a lesson holds the video and its metadata. For each lesson the instructor sets:

    Title What the lesson is called in the outline.
    Duration In minutes — used for the course's total runtime and to decide when watching counts as complete.
    Video The lesson file. Uploads go to the configured lesson media disk and report progress.
    Preview Marks the lesson as watchable before purchase, which is the strongest argument a course page can make.
    Order Position within the section; sections and lessons both drag into the order they should be taught in.

    Attachments

    Any lesson can carry downloadable resources alongside the video: slides, exercise files, datasets, or a link to an external repository. An attachment is either an uploaded file or an external URL, and uploaded files keep their title, type, and size so students know what they are getting. Each attachment records how many times it has been downloaded, and downloads are authorized per student, so a link copied out of the classroom does not work for anyone else.

    Building the quiz

    The quiz builder sits next to the curriculum for each course. It writes the assessment that unlocks that course's certificate.

    • Settings — the quiz title, the pass mark as a percentage, and a maximum number of attempts, which can be left unlimited.
    • Questions — each question has its own text, a point value, and an optional explanation that students see when they review a marked attempt.
    • Options — two or more answers per question with one or more marked correct, written in the order students should read them.
    • Ordering — questions and options are both reorderable, so a quiz can be arranged from easy to hard.

    Grading happens on the server: the student's answers are scored against the stored correct options, the attempt is saved with the answers as submitted, and the result is the percentage that decides whether the certificate is unlocked.

    Earnings

    An instructor is paid per sale, not per month, and the ledger is precise about the arithmetic.

    Field Meaning
    Gross amount What the student paid for that item.
    Commission The site's percentage at the moment of the sale, stored on the record so later changes to the rate never rewrite history.
    Net amount Gross minus commission — what the instructor earned.
    Available amount How much of that earning is still unrequested. An earning requested in a payout keeps the remainder available.
    Status Available, requested, paid, or reversed.

    A reversed earning — a refunded order, for example — is kept in the ledger rather than deleted, so the history always explains the current balance. Because earnings are recorded per order item, a multi-course order pays each instructor for exactly their own course.

    Payout requests

    Withdrawing earnings is a request, not an automatic transfer. An instructor asks; an administrator pays out of band and marks it paid.

    1. A request is only possible above the minimum

      The minimum amount comes from the panel's payout settings. Below it, the form refuses the request and states the threshold.

    2. Partial amounts are allowed

      An instructor does not have to withdraw everything at once. A request can draw part of a single earning, and whatever is left stays available for a later request — so a large sale can be paid out gradually.

    3. A method and its details are chosen

      The available methods come from the panel, and each one has its own fields for the account to be paid into, for example a bank transfer or an online wallet. The details are stored with the request so an administrator paying it sees exactly where the money should go.

    4. The request is reviewed

      It waits in Payments → Payout Requests. Approving it reserves the earnings; marking it paid completes the ledger; rejecting it with a reason releases the earnings back to available, so the instructor can request again.

    The instructor's earnings area shows the balance, every request with its status, and the reason for any rejection.
